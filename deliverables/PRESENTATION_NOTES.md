# Presentation speaker notes

## Slide 1: Transparent, wallet-controlled savings
Introduce the student identity and sponsor-funded savings scope. Do not describe this as a bank or a production investment product.

## Slide 2: Can members verify the rules?
Explain why an operator spreadsheet cannot independently enforce custody rules. Label the use case as proposed rather than interviewed.

## Slide 3: Context supports investigation, not adoption claims
Cite report references 1 and 2. Explain the limited applicability of global inclusion statistics to an existing-wallet target group.

## Slide 4: Identity and custody are separate
Walk through the browser, MetaMask, API, SQLite and vault. A password session controls portal access; wallet signatures control funds.

## Slide 5: Four business transaction types
Show the single implemented contract. At five percent simple interest, one ETH over a 365-day year earns 0.05 ETH. Reserve exhaustion delays interest claims.

## Slide 6: Sign, confirm, verify, record
Run the customer demonstration. Inspect the MetaMask network, destination and gas fee. If the transaction is pending, preserve its hash and use a clearly labelled earlier receipt.

## Slide 7: Local checks pass; scope is bounded
Use captured logs in evidence/. Explain that assertion counts are not independent test counts. Simulated time is not real Sepolia elapsed time.

## Slide 8: Optimizer comparison, measured locally
Explain optimizer off/on, 200 runs, compiler 0.8.30, Shanghai target and identical operation sequence. Fees depend on gas price. See testing report.

## Slide 9: Principal protection is only one control
Discuss public ledger privacy and email-to-wallet linkage. Passing tests do not establish audited safety or sustainable funding.

## Slide 10: Docker-ready; public evidence is pending
Show the actual deployment register. Do not substitute localhost for the required public URL. Show real explorer evidence only if available.

## Slide 11: Focused scope, transparent limits
Explain the agreed flexible scope and practical improvements. Retain the original-count comparison without adding unrelated functionality.

## Slide 12: An inspectable prototype, with a clear next step
Conclude the ten-minute demonstration. Answer questions from actual evidence, identify limitations and do not invent contribution history.
